### Coaches Corner — Last Week's Observations

I've been reviewing our practices and games, and I can see you're working hard. So that's not entirely the issue. The issue is where we're working hard.

Too many of our shifts end with us chasing the play—reaching for pucks, catching up to passes, and arriving second to loose pucks in the corner. Yes, sometimes we get outworked. But just as often, it's a lack of positioning. Players who win the most puck battles aren't necessarily the fastest or strongest. They're the ones who seal off their opponent, initiate contact, and put their body between the other guy and the puck *before* it gets there. Watch the clips in the *Positioning Before Possession* article. The separation happens in the approach, not the collision.

The same goes for our offensive zone. We're making it hard on ourselves by standing still or skating in straight lines. If you want more touches, you have to make the passer's job easier. That means facing the play, timing your speed, and taking back ice instead of getting ahead of the puck. The *Puck Acquisition* piece explains how small route adjustments—folding under, rolling speed into the play—create clean lanes and better angles. It's not about skating harder. It's about skating smarter so the puck finds *you*.

And for our D corps: your partner is your lifeline. When you're retrieving pucks in our end, scan first. Protect yourself, use the net, and skate hard to the puck. Then protect your partner—communicate pressure, be an easy outlet, and create obstacles for their forecheckers. The *Defensive Partner Work* article shows how two players working in sync can neutralize even the toughest forecheck. One guy goes to work; the other makes sure he's not doing it alone.

Read the three pieces below and bring one thing to practice this week that you're going to change. We'll build from there.

### This Week's 3 Focus

**Theme:** Positioning, Pace, Protecting the House 

Everything we're working on this week rolls up into three themes. 
1. **Positioning** — win the battle before the puck arrives. Seal your man, initiate contact, and put your body between him and the puck so the race is over before it starts. 
2. **Pace** — timing beats speed. Stop standing still and skating straight lines; face the play, take back ice, fold under, and roll into your routes so the puck finds you in stride. 
3. **Protect the House** — nothing gets through the middle of our end without a fight. D partners: scan before you touch the puck, use the net, talk to each other, and be the outlet your partner needs. Everyone else: track back through the middle and make our slot the hardest ice on the rink to enter. Nail these three things and the rest of the game takes care of itself.
