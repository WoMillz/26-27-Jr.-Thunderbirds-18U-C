## Coaches’ Corner — Building on Last Week

Last week, we talked about positioning before possession—winning the battle before the puck arrives, sealing your man, and taking back ice so the puck finds you. I saw flashes of it. Our retrievals got cleaner when our D scanned first and used the net.

Now we build the next layer: **what happens after we win that puck.**

A great defensive play that ends in a panic flip or a turnover at our blue line is wasted work. Breakouts are where positioning turns into offense. But here’s the problem: right now, our breakouts are improvised. One guy goes one way, the other guesses, and suddenly we’re disconnected and chasing again.

That’s the thread from last week: **chasing happens when we’re not predictable to each other.**

The best breakouts aren’t the fanciest. They’re the ones where all five skaters know what’s happening before it happens. That means three things: Be **connected**—support the puck carrier with options. Be **organized**—know your route and run it. Be **predictable**—so your teammate can make the play without guessing.

The “Creating Predictability in Your Breakouts” piece below lays out the five ingredients: hard back to the puck, play fast on first touch, middle support, win the walls, and push the pace. Every one of these is a positioning and pace concept we’ve already started working on—now we’re applying them to the most important 20 seconds of every shift.

Read it. Watch the drills. Bring one breakout habit to practice this week.

## This Week’s 3 Focus Areas

**Theme: Connected, Organized, Predictable**

Everything this week rolls up into making our breakouts automatic, so we spend less time defending and more time attacking.

1. **Connected**—hard back to the puck. Support your D partner and your centerman. Give the puck carrier at least two options every time, not one. If you’re the closest forward, be under the puck, not beside it.
2. **Organized**—play fast on first touch. Know what you’re doing with the puck before it gets to your stick. D: scan, retrieve, and have your outlet decided. Forwards: run your route—swing low, fill the middle, or win your wall—and commit to it.
3. **Predictable**—talk and trust. Call your routes. If your teammate knows where you’re going, he can put the puck there before you arrive. That’s not magic—it’s communication plus repetition. Predictability is what turns five individuals into a unit that breaks out cleanly under pressure.

Nail these three, and we’ll stop gifting teams extra offensive-zone time. We’ll go from surviving in our end to owning the transition.
